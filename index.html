02 / PROOF BEFORE CLAIMS

Our evidence standard

A dramatic rumor isn't a finding. And a broad corporate policy shouldn't be mistaken for proof that a specific title uses a specific algorithm.

01

Document the statement

Record an official policy, product document, reproducible technical test, or attributable first-hand evidence. Include a stable URL and review date.

02

Label the scope

Is the evidence about a platform, company, service, game, or specific technical system? Never promote one level of evidence to another.

03

State the limit

Each record contains both what the evidence supports and what remains unknown. Absence of evidence isn't evidence of absence.

04

Invite reproducibility

Publish redacted test protocols, timestamps, environmental details and hashes. Protect usernames, access tokens, bystanders and minors.

Evidence labels

Documented game feature

An official source describes a capability in a named game. Does not establish every internal data flow.

Official disclosure

A public policy or agreement states categories or possible uses at platform/publisher scope.

Context only

Relevant corporate/platform link but no direct telemetry conclusion about the named game.

Unverified allegation

Someone has made a claim. It must not be counted as a confirmed deployment.

03 / REPRODUCIBLE FIELD LAB

Observe, don't assume.

Want to audit a game you own? Start with destination metadata and compare network connections during a controlled test. Never publish an unredacted capture or bypass anti-cheat.

  1. Define the scope. Write down the exact title, version, platform, account type and test date.
  2. Establish baseline. Record idle-system network activity before opening the launcher.
  3. Capture safely. Observe connections using Windows Resource Monitor or Wireshark on systems you control. Avoid decrypted login/session data.
  4. Compare phases. Repeat menu, idle-game, active-play and shutdown sessions while noting launcher and OS traffic.
  5. Document uncertainty. Domains and byte counts cannot prove encrypted payload meaning or server-side inferences.
  6. Redact before sharing. Remove IPs, IDs, usernames, tokens, chat/audio, precise location and information about other people.
